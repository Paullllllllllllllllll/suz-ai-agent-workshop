# Summary: Helfer, Grossmann, and Osikominu (2023)

Helfer, Fabienne, Volker Grossmann, and Aderonke Osikominu. 2023. "How Does
Immigration Affect Housing Costs in Switzerland?" *Swiss Journal of Economics
and Statistics* 159 (1): 5. <https://doi.org/10.1186/s41937-023-00110-1>.

## Question

What is the short-run causal effect of immigration on the prices of
owner-occupied housing and on rents across Swiss regions (p. 1)?

## Data

Wüest Partner hedonic transaction-price indices for single-family homes and
owner-occupied apartments across 106 local labor markets (MS-regions),
1985-2016, plus a cantonal asking-price rental index for 1998-2016; population
and foreign-resident stocks from the Federal Statistical Office (ESPOP and
STATPOP), with the 1980 federal census supplying historical settlement
patterns (pp. 6-7).

## Method

Two strategies, both exploiting the Agreement on the Free Movement of Persons
(AFMP) with the EU, in force from 2002, as an exogenous immigration shock. The
first instruments current regional immigrant inflows with a Card-style
shift-share instrument built on 1980 settlement patterns and interacts the
inflow with a post-2002 dummy, so the post- versus pre-reform differential
reads as a difference-in-differences estimate of the reform (pp. 2, 7-9). The
second is an event study grouping MS-regions by their historical share of
EU-15 immigrants in 1980 (pp. 10-11).

## Main findings

After the reform, an annual inflow of foreigners equal to 1% of the initial
population raises single-family home prices by 4.3% and owner-occupied
apartment prices by 5.9%; before the reform, immigration has no effect on
house prices (p. 3). At the cantonal level the same inflow raises rents by
7.4% for 1998-2016 and by 8% for 2002-2016 — more than the prices of
owner-occupied housing (p. 3). The event study shows that moving from a
region with a historically low or medium EU-15 immigrant stock to a high one
raises annual house price growth by about one percentage point after 2002
(pp. 3, 17). Measuring the inflow by net migration instead of the change in
the foreigner stock yields smaller but still significant effects (2.2% for
rents; 1.8% and 2.4% for home and apartment prices, p. 3).

## Limitations

The estimates are short-run demand effects that hinge on housing supply being
fixed in the short run (p. 4). Cantonal regressions rest on only 26 clusters,
so the authors report wild-bootstrap p-values, and the cantonal first stages
are weaker than the MS-level ones (pp. 8, 15). Immigration is measured by the
change in the stock of foreign nationals, which conflates inflows with births,
deaths, and naturalizations of foreigners (pp. 10, 21).
